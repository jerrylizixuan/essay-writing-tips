Overview

All Essays: Standard Format

  • Introduction: Hook → Background → Thesis
  • Body paragraphs: Topic sentence → Point → Example/Evidence → Explain → Repeat for each point
  • Conclusion: Restate thesis → Summarize points → Final concluding sentence

General Writing Tips

  • Brainstorm (storm your topic) before writing.
  • Make an outline so your ideas stay organized.
  • Write a strong thesis first to improve clarity and structure.
  • Each paragraph should focus on one main idea.

Essay Types (Fold / Unfold)
Descriptive Essay ▼

What it is

Descriptive essays describe objects, people, events, and feelings. The goal is to help the reader picture exactly what you describe.

Tips

  • Use the five senses (sight, sound, smell, taste, touch).
  • Be specific (strong nouns + verbs, not vague adjectives).
  • Use figurative language carefully (don’t overdo it).
  • Include origin/background (what led up to it, if it’s an event).
  • Connect to the reader’s emotions.
Narrative Essay ▼

What it is

Narrative (personal) writing tells a story from the author’s point of view, usually with a reason/message behind it.

Key features

  • Characters
  • Setting
  • Main event / climax
  • Clear storyline
  • Written in first person or third person

Tips

  • Focus on developing characters.
  • Use descriptive techniques: senses, emotions, strategic dialogue, thoughts.
  • Make sure the story has a clear point.
Argumentative Essay ▼

What it is

Argumentative writing tries to convince readers to support a claim using evidence (not just opinion).

Tips

  • Have a clear thesis.
  • Use strong evidence: facts, statistics, examples.
  • Use persuasive strategies:
    • Ethos: credibility / trust
    • Logos: logic / facts
    • Pathos: emotion
  • Include a strong conclusion.
  • Research well.
Expository Essay ▼

What it is

Expository writing explains a topic clearly and logically. It informs using facts rather than personal opinions.

Tips

  • Use facts, examples, and explain evidence.
  • Use concise and clear language.
  • Organize ideas logically.
  • Avoid descriptive/figurative language (keep it objective).
  • Compare-and-contrast essays are often expository.
